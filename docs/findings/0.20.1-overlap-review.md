# Core 0.20.1: retrieval scoring candidate

**Status: implementation candidate; release review remains open.** Core commit
`e2323cb54ab00bad0b02689296fc79b8363f9a0c` extends candidate `28ed70c` by
reusing a single query/candidate token overlap to calculate text coverage and
cue fit. The arithmetic, filtering, weights and ranking are unchanged. The
cache lives only for the calculation; no text or tenant data persists in a
cross-retrieval cache. The preceding [candidate review](0.20.1-candidate-review.md)
retains its measured SQLite p95 miss and the subsequent passing repeat.

## Candidate measurements

On the user-selected 2018 Mac, CPython 3.11.15 and SQLite 3.53.1, the unchanged
mixed fixture was run sequentially and uninstrumented. At 100 and 250 base
observations, the table reports the median of 100 recalls after the first. At
1,000, it reports nearest-rank p95 across 100 recalls including the first. The
fixture adds six correction/withdrawal observations. Raw timings and rounded
outputs are retained in `data/0.20.1-overlap-{memory,sqlite}-{100,250,1000}.json`.

| Base observations | Memory | SQLite | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100, warm median | 0.079 s | 0.119 s | under 1 s |
| 250, warm median | 0.202 s | 0.302 s | under 1 s |
| 1,000, p95 | 1.293 s | 1.920 s | under 2 s |

All ranked outputs rounded to eight decimals, counts and mixed-operation counts
match between the two backends for every query. Both backends meet the selected
targets in these runs. The host was not isolated, OS caches were uncontrolled,
and the workload is synthetic. The preceding candidate's SQLite runs measured
2.919 and 1.955 seconds p95, respectively, and must not be discarded; the
latest 1.920-second measurement has limited margin and is not a production SLA
or a cold-device study. Start and finish host load averages were 2.75/3.22/3.55
and 2.50/2.93/3.25 (one/five/fifteen minutes).
